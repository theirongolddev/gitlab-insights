# Developer Interview Template

**Purpose**: Understand developer workflows, pain points, and how GitLab data could help solve common problems.

**Format**: 1-on-1 conversation, 30-45 minutes
**Tone**: Casual and conversational - this is a chat, not an interrogation

______________________________________________________________________

## Before You Start

**Interviewer prep:**

- Review the person's GitLab activity (projects, recent MRs, review patterns)
- Have a notebook or doc ready to capture quotes and observations
- Record the session (with permission) so you can focus on listening

**Opening script:**

> "Hey, thanks for taking the time to chat with me. I'm trying to understand how developers here actually work day-to-day - what's working, what's frustrating, and where there might be opportunities to make things better. There are no right or wrong answers, and anything you share stays between us. Feel free to be brutally honest - that's actually the most helpful."

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 1: Context (3 min)

*Goal: Establish baseline, determine if they have external reference points*

1. **How long have you been with the team, and what kind of work do you primarily do?** (frontend, backend, infra, etc.)

2. **Is this your first dev role, or have you worked elsewhere before?**

   - *If yes: What was the environment like there? (Startup, enterprise, agency, etc.)*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 2: How You Actually Work (7 min)

*Goal: Understand real workflows, not the idealized version*

3. **Walk me through a typical day. What does it actually look like from when you start working to when you wrap up?**

   - *Probe: Where do you start? What tools do you open first?*
   - *Probe: How much of your day is heads-down coding vs other stuff?*

4. **When you need to understand why something was built a certain way - the history or reasoning behind a decision - how do you find out?**

   - *Probe: Where do you look first? Second?*
   - *Probe: How often do you actually find the answer vs give up?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 3: The "Product Manager Hat" (10 min)

*Goal: Understand time spent on non-coding activities - THIS IS THE CORE*

5. **If you had to put a number on it, what percentage of your time goes to actual coding vs everything else?**

   - *Probe: What's the "everything else"?*
   - *Probe: What about finding information vs doing the actual work?*

6. **How often do you find yourself doing work that feels like it should be someone else's job?** Things like:

   - Figuring out requirements or specs
   - Getting stakeholder alignment
   - Making design decisions
   - Writing documentation that doesn't exist yet
   - Chasing down answers from other teams

   *Probe: Can you give me a recent example?*

7. **When you start a new task or feature, how clear are the requirements usually?**

   - *Probe: What percentage of the time do you have to go back and clarify things?*
   - *Probe: At what point do you usually discover the requirements weren't complete?*

8. **What information do you wish you had at the start of a task that you usually don't?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 4: Code Review (8 min)

*Goal: Understand the review process and where requirements actually get finalized*

9. **Walk me through what happens when you put up an MR. What's that process like from submission to merge?**

   - *Probe: How long does it typically take?*
   - *Probe: How many rounds of feedback is normal?*

10. **How often do significant changes - not just code style, but actual feature or design changes - come up during code review?**

    - *Probe: Can you think of a recent example?*
    - *Probe: At what point do you think those changes should have been caught?*

11. **On the flip side, when you're reviewing someone else's code, what's that experience like?**

    - *Probe: Do you have enough context to review effectively?*
    - *Probe: What information would help you give better reviews?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 5: Pain Points (5 min)

*Goal: Identify the biggest friction points*

12. **What's something that should take 5 minutes but regularly takes an hour?**

    - *Probe: How often does this happen?*

13. **Is there anything you actively avoid doing because it's too painful or tedious?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 6: GitLab & Information Needs (8 min)

*Goal: Understand what a GitLab-based tool could solve*

14. **What information do you wish GitLab surfaced more easily?**

    - *Examples: Who to ask about a piece of code, why a decision was made, what's blocking something*

15. **If you could ask any question about your codebase or team's work and get an instant answer, what would you ask?**

    - *Probe: What would you do with that information?*

16. **How do you currently track what's happening across the team or project?**

    - *Probe: What's missing from that picture?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 7: Wrap-up (2 min)

17. **If I could snap my fingers and fix one thing about how you work, what would it be?**

18. **Is there anything we didn't talk about that you think I should know?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Closing

> "This was really helpful, thanks for being so open. Based on what we learn from these conversations, we're hoping to build some tools that actually help. If something comes up later that you think is relevant, feel free to ping me."

## Tips for Interviewers

1. **Listen more than you talk.** Aim for 80/20 - they should be talking 80% of the time.

2. **Use silence.** If they pause, don't jump in. Let them think - they'll often add something valuable.

3. **Follow the thread.** If they mention something interesting, dig deeper. The script is a guide, not a straitjacket.

4. **Capture exact quotes.** Their words are more powerful than your paraphrasing.

5. **Watch for emotions.** Frustration, excitement, resignation - these signal what really matters to them.

6. **Don't solve problems in the interview.** You're here to understand, not fix. Save solutions for later.

7. **Ask "why" and "can you give me an example" liberally.** These unlock the real insights.
