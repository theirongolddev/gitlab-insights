# Engineer Growth & Development Survey

**Purpose**: Understand how engineers learn and grow, identify gaps in development support, and discover appetite for mentorship and structure.

**Estimated time**: 10-12 minutes
**Anonymity**: All responses are anonymous. Be honest - that's what helps us most.

---

## Survey Introduction

> We're trying to understand how engineers here learn and grow - what's working, what's not, and what we could do better. This isn't about evaluating anyone - it's about understanding the environment so we can improve it.
>
> Your honest feedback matters.

## Survey Implementation Notes

### Analysis Framework

**Segment responses by:**
- Q1 (level) - Junior vs senior perspectives differ significantly
- Q3 (experience count) - First-job vs experienced have different baselines
- Q4 (previous orgs had programs) - Critical context for expectations

**Key metrics to track:**

*Skills Utilization:*
- Q5: % who feel underutilized
- Q6: What people could contribute but aren't

*How Growth Happens:*
- Q7: Self-directed vs supported (high self-directed = no structure)
- Q8: % who've had active help
- Q9: Intentional vs accidental growth

*Barriers & Needs:*
- Q10: Top barriers by frequency
- Q11: What investment would mean

*Career Clarity:*
- Q12: % with unclear/nonexistent path
- Q13: Level distinction clarity
- Q14: What determines seniority (skill vs tenure vs early employee)
- Q15: Conversation frequency

*Mentorship:*
- Q16: Supply (who mentors) vs demand (who wants to)
- Q17: What would help mentors
- Q18: Support system existence
- Q19: Knowledge sharing mechanisms

*Culture:*
- Q20: Agreement matrix
- Q21: What happens when you learn vs ship

**Red flags to watch for:**
- High % selecting "I haven't developed much" (Q7) = stagnation
- High % selecting "No - figured it out on my own" (Q8) = no support
- "No time" as top barrier (Q10) = prioritization problem
- High % unclear on career path (Q12) = no framework
- Seniority based on tenure/early employee (Q14) = not skill-based
- High % "No but I'd like to" mentor (Q16) = latent supply blocked
- Low scores on Q20 agreement items

**Cross-cuts to analyze:**
- Q5 (utilization) by Q1 (level) and Q3 (experience) - are experienced people underutilized?
- Q8 (had help) by Q9 (intentional growth) - does support lead to intentionality?
- Q14 (what determines senior) by Q1 (level) - do seniors and juniors see it differently?
- Q16 (mentors) by Q1 (level) - who's mentoring and who's not?

### Building the Case

To make the case for investment:
1. "X% of engineers have had no active development support"
2. "X% feel their skills are underutilized" (especially experienced hires)
3. "The top barrier is Y, cited by Z% of respondents"
4. "X% want to mentor but don't - latent capacity exists"
5. "Seniority is perceived as tenure-based by X% - not skill-based"
6. "Only X% have clear career paths"

### Surfacing Structural Opportunities

Look for signals that people would welcome structure:
- High % wanting to mentor (Q16) but no program
- High % selecting "formal mentorship program" in Q11
- Low scores on "clear expectations for each level" (Q20)
- High % selecting structure-related items in Q11
