# Developer Experience Interview Template

**Purpose**: Understand developer workflows, pain points, visibility into work, and identify opportunities for structural improvements.

**Format**: 1-on-1 conversation, 30-45 minutes
**Tone**: Casual and conversational - this is a chat, not an interrogation

______________________________________________________________________

## Before You Start

**Interviewer prep:**

- Review the person's GitLab activity (projects, recent MRs, review patterns)
- Note their tenure and any context about their background
- Record the session (with permission) so you can focus on listening

**Opening script:**

> "Hey, thanks for taking the time to chat with me. I'm trying to understand how developers here actually work day-to-day - what's working, what's frustrating, and where there might be opportunities to make things better. There are no right or wrong answers, and anything you share stays between us. Feel free to be brutally honest - that's actually the most helpful."

## Section 1: Background & Context (5 min)

*Goal: Understand their frame of reference - critical for interpreting responses*

1. **How long have you been here, and what's your current role?**

2. **Is this your first engineering job, or have you worked elsewhere before?**

   - *If elsewhere: How many places? What were they like? (Size, structure, type of company)*
   - *If first job: What did you expect engineering work to be like before you started?*

3. **How does working here compare to your expectations or previous experience?**

   - *For experienced: What's similar? What's different?*
   - *For first-timers: What's surprised you?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 2: How Work Happens (8 min)

*Goal: Understand the actual workflow and surface visibility gaps*

4. **Walk me through how work gets to you. How do you know what to work on?**

   - *Probe: Where does that information come from?*
   - *Probe: How far ahead can you see what's coming?*

5. **Do you know why you're working on the things you're working on?** How does your work connect to bigger goals?

   - *Probe: Is that connection clear, or do you have to piece it together?*
   - *Probe: Do you know what the company/product priorities are right now?*

6. **How much visibility do you have into what other engineers are working on?**

   - *Probe: How would you find out if you wanted to know?*
   - *Probe: Has there ever been duplicated work or surprised dependencies?*

7. **When you need to understand context on something - why code is a certain way, or why a decision was made - how do you find out?**

   - *Probe: Where do you look first? Who do you ask?*
   - *Probe: How often do you actually find the answer?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 3: Time & Non-Coding Work (8 min)

*Goal: Quantify time allocation and PM-like work*

8. **If you had to put a number on it, what percentage of your time goes to actual coding vs everything else?**

   - *Probe: What's the "everything else"?*
   - *Probe: How much time goes to just figuring out what to build or finding information?*

9. **How often do you find yourself doing work that feels like it should be someone else's job?** Things like:

   - Figuring out requirements
   - Making design decisions
   - Coordinating with other people
   - Tracking or reporting on your own work

   *Probe: Can you give me a recent example?*

10. **When you start a new task, how clear are the requirements?**

    - *Probe: What percentage of the time do you have to go back and clarify?*
    - *Probe: At what point do you usually discover requirements were incomplete?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 4: Code Review (6 min)

*Goal: Understand the review process, bottlenecks, and gatekeeping*

11. **Walk me through what happens when you put up an MR. What's that process like?**

    - *Probe: How long does it typically take to get merged?*
    - *Probe: Who reviews your code? Is that consistent?*

12. **How often do significant changes - not just style, but actual feature or design changes - come up during code review?**

    - *Probe: When do you think those issues should have been caught?*

13. **Do you do code reviews yourself?**

    - *If yes: How did that start? Do you feel like you have enough context to review well?*
    - *If no: Is that something you'd want to do? What's stopped that from happening?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 5: Collaboration & Knowledge (5 min)

*Goal: Surface silos and knowledge-sharing gaps*

14. **If you had a question about a part of the codebase you've never touched, what would you do?**

    - *Probe: Is it clear who knows what around here?*
    - *Probe: How did you learn what you know about who to ask?*

15. **How do you learn about what's happening on the team?** New features, technical decisions, changes?

    - *Probe: Is there a regular mechanism for that, or does it happen ad-hoc?*

16. **Have you ever been surprised by something another engineer built or changed?** Something you wish you'd known about earlier?

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 6: Pain Points (5 min)

*Goal: Identify concrete friction*

17. **What's something that should take 5 minutes but regularly takes an hour?**

18. **What's the most frustrating part of your day-to-day work?**

    - *Probe: How often does this happen?*
    - *Probe: What would it look like if this was fixed?*

19. **Is there anything you actively avoid doing because it's too painful?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 7: Structure & Improvements (5 min)

*Goal: Surface appetite for change and potential solutions*

20. **If you could change one thing about how work happens here, what would have the biggest impact?**

21. **Would it help to have more structure around how work is planned or coordinated?** What would that look like to you?

    - *Let them answer first - don't lead*
    - *Probe if needed: Teams? Regular planning? Someone coordinating across projects?*

22. **Is there anything we didn't talk about that you think I should know?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Closing

> "This was really helpful, thanks for being so open. I'm trying to understand patterns across the team and figure out what we could do better. If something comes up later that you think is relevant, feel free to ping me."

## Tips for Interviewers

1. **Segment responses by experience.** First-job engineers can't compare - their "normal" is all they know. Weight responses from people with outside experience differently.

2. **Listen for normalization.** "That's just how it is" or "I've gotten used to it" signals learned helplessness, not satisfaction.

3. **Probe the visibility gaps.** Many problems stem from not knowing what's happening. Surface this gently.

4. **Don't lead on structure questions.** Ask what would help before suggesting teams/coordination. See if they arrive there themselves.

5. **Watch for resignation vs frustration.** Frustration means they want change. Resignation means they've given up.

6. **Note who they mention.** If everyone goes to the same 2 people for answers, that's a bottleneck and a bus factor.

7. **Capture exact quotes.** "I have no idea what anyone else is working on" is powerful data.
