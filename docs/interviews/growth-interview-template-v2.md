# Engineer Growth & Development Interview Template

**Purpose**: Understand how engineers learn and grow (or don't), identify gaps in development support, and discover appetite for mentorship and structure.

**Format**: 1-on-1 conversation, 30-40 minutes
**Tone**: Casual and conversational - this is a chat, not a performance review

______________________________________________________________________

## Before You Start

**Interviewer prep:**

- Know the person's tenure and any background on their experience level
- Be prepared for this to surface frustrations - growth is often a sore spot
- Record the session (with permission) so you can focus on listening

**Opening script:**

> "Hey, thanks for taking the time. I'm trying to understand how engineers here learn and grow - what's working, what's not, and what we could do better. This isn't about evaluating you - it's about understanding the environment. Be as honest as you want."

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 1: Background & Context (5 min)

*Goal: Understand their frame of reference for what development looks like*

1. **How long have you been here, and what's your current role/level?**

2. **Is this your first engineering job, or have you worked elsewhere?**

   - *If elsewhere: How did those places handle engineer development? Any training, mentoring, formal programs?*
   - *If first job: What did you expect in terms of learning and growth when you started?*

3. **Do you feel like your skills and experience are fully utilized here?**

   - *Probe: Are there things you could contribute that you're not given the opportunity to?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 2: How Growth Has Happened (7 min)

*Goal: Understand what learning has actually occurred and how*

4. **What skills have you developed most since you've been here?**

   - *Probe: How did that happen? Formal training? Figured it out yourself? Learned from someone?*

5. **Think of something you're better at now than when you started. How did you get better at it?**

   - *Probe: Was that intentional or did it just happen?*
   - *Probe: Did anyone help you, or was it mostly solo?*

6. **Has anyone here actively helped you grow - formally or informally?**

   - *If yes: What did that look like? How did it start?*
   - *If no: Would you have wanted that? What do you think stopped it from happening?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 3: What's Missing (7 min)

*Goal: Identify gaps and latent demand for development support*

7. **What do you want to get better at that you haven't had the chance to develop?**

   - *Probe: What's gotten in the way?*
   - *Probe: Do you know how you'd develop that skill if you had the time/support?*

8. **When you're stuck on something - technically or professionally - what do you do?**

   - *Probe: Is there someone you go to? How did that relationship form?*
   - *Probe: What happens when you can't figure it out?*

9. **What would "the company investing in your growth" actually look like to you?**

   - *Let them answer first, then probe if needed: Training? Mentorship? Time? Conference budget? Career conversations?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 4: Career Path & Levels (5 min)

*Goal: Understand if there's clarity on progression and expectations*

10. **Do you know what the next step in your career here looks like?**

    - *Probe: Is that clear? Written down anywhere?*
    - *Probe: Do you know what you'd need to do to get there?*

11. **Are the distinctions between levels or roles clear to you?** What makes someone senior vs not senior here?

    - *Probe: Is that based on skill? Tenure? Something else?*

12. **Have you had conversations with anyone about your growth or career trajectory?**

    - *Probe: How often? What comes out of those conversations?*
    - *Probe: Do those conversations lead to anything concrete?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 5: Mentoring & Knowledge Sharing (7 min)

*Goal: Understand mentorship supply/demand and knowledge flow*

13. **Do you mentor or help develop other engineers? Formally or informally?**

    - *If yes: How did that start? Is it recognized or expected? What would help you do it better?*
    - *If no: Is that something you'd want to do? What would make it possible?*

14. **When someone more junior is struggling, what typically happens?**

    - *Probe: Do people step in? Is there a system for this?*
    - *Probe: Is helping others recognized or valued here?*

15. **How does knowledge get shared around here?** When someone learns something or makes a decision, how do others find out?

    - *Probe: Are there any regular mechanisms for this? Tech talks, docs, anything?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 6: Culture & Environment (5 min)

*Goal: Understand structural and cultural barriers*

16. **Does the culture here encourage or discourage taking time to learn?**

    - *Probe: What signals that?*
    - *Probe: What happens if you take time to learn something instead of shipping?*

17. **Do you feel like there's room to take on more responsibility here if you wanted to?**

    - *Probe: What would that look like? Who would you talk to?*
    - *Probe: Is the path to more responsibility clear?*

18. **If you could change one thing about how this place develops engineers, what would it be?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 7: Wrap-up (2 min)

19. **If someone joined tomorrow at your level, what would you tell them about growing their career here?**

20. **Anything else I should know?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Closing

> "This was really helpful. We're trying to figure out how to actually invest in engineers here, and understanding the current state is the first step. If anything else comes to mind, feel free to reach out."

## Tips for Interviewers

1. **Segment by experience.** First-job engineers may not know what development support looks like. They can't miss what they've never seen.

2. **Listen for "that's just how it is."** This signals normalization, not satisfaction.

3. **Probe the "skills utilization" question.** For experienced hires especially - are they being used to their potential?

4. **Note who mentions wanting to mentor.** This is latent supply that could be activated with structure.

5. **Watch for frustration about levels.** If distinctions feel arbitrary or tenure-based, that's a structural gap.

6. **Listen for time as a barrier.** "I'd love to learn/mentor but there's no time" is a prioritization/culture problem.

7. **Don't promise anything.** You're gathering data, not committing to solutions.
