# Developer Interview Template

**Purpose**: Understand developer workflows, pain points, and how GitLab data could help solve common problems.

**Format**: 1-on-1 conversation, 45-60 minutes
**Tone**: Casual and conversational - this is a chat, not an interrogation

______________________________________________________________________

## Before You Start

**Interviewer prep:**

- Review the person's GitLab activity if possible (projects they work on, recent MRs)
- Have a notebook or doc ready to capture quotes and observations
- Record the session (with permission) so you can focus on listening

**Opening script:**

> "Hey, thanks for taking the time to chat with me. I'm trying to understand how developers here actually work day-to-day - what's working, what's frustrating, and where there might be opportunities to make things better. There are no right or wrong answers, and anything you share stays between us. Feel free to be brutally honest - that's actually the most helpful."

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 1: Background & Context (5 min)

*Goal: Build rapport and understand their role*

1. **How would you describe your role here? How long have you been with the team?**

2. **What kind of work do you primarily do?** (frontend, backend, infrastructure, full-stack, etc.)

3. **What projects or repos do you spend most of your time in?**

4. **Is this your first dev role, or have you worked elsewhere before?**

   - *If yes: What was the environment like there? (Startup, enterprise, agency, etc.)*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 2: A Day in the Life (10 min)

*Goal: Understand their actual workflows, not the idealized version*

5. **Walk me through a typical day. What does it actually look like from when you start working to when you wrap up?**

   - *Probe: Where do you start? What tools do you open first?*
   - *Probe: How much of your day is heads-down coding vs other stuff?*

6. **How do you decide what to work on each day? Where does that information come from?**

   - *Probe: Is it clear what's most important? Or do you have to figure that out yourself?*

7. **When you need to understand context on something - a feature, a codebase, a decision that was made - where do you go?**

   - *Probe: GitLab issues? Teams? Ask someone? Code comments? Docs?*

8. **How does your day-to-day here compare to what you expected when you started?**

   - *Probe: What parts of the job took the longest to get comfortable with?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 3: The "Product Manager Hat" (10 min)

*Goal: Understand time spent on non-coding activities*

9. **How much of your time would you say is spent on actual coding vs everything else?**

   - *Probe: What's the "everything else"?*

10. **How often do you find yourself doing work that feels like it should be someone else's job?** Things like:

    - Figuring out requirements or specs
    - Getting stakeholder alignment
    - Making design decisions
    - Writing documentation that doesn't exist yet
    - Chasing down answers from other teams

    *Probe: Can you give me a recent example?*
    *Probe: How did you learn to do this kind of work?*

11. **When you start a new task or feature, how clear are the requirements usually?**

    - *Probe: What percentage of the time do you have to go back and clarify things?*
    - *Probe: What does that clarification process look like?*

12. **What information do you wish you had at the start of a task that you usually don't?**

13. **When it comes to scoping or planning work, what's been your learning curve? What would help you feel more confident in that area?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 4: Pain Points & Frustrations (10 min)

*Goal: Identify the biggest problems - let them vent*

14. **What's the most frustrating part of your day-to-day work?**

    - *Probe: How often does this happen?*
    - *Probe: What would it look like if this was fixed?*

15. **What slows you down the most?** Think about things like:

    - Waiting on others
    - Context switching
    - Tooling issues
    - Unclear requirements
    - Finding information
    - Code reviews taking too long

16. **Is there anything you avoid doing because it's too painful or tedious?**

17. **What's something that should take 5 minutes but actually takes an hour?**

18. **What parts of the job have had the steepest learning curve?**

    - *Probe: What helped you get better at those things?*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 5: GitLab Usage (10 min)

*Goal: Understand how they use GitLab and what insights might help*

19. **How would you describe your GitLab usage?** (basic, intermediate, power user)

    - *Probe: What features do you use regularly?*
    - *Probe: What features do you know exist but don't use? Why not?*

20. **When you're in GitLab, what are you typically trying to do?**

    - *Examples: Create MRs, review code, check pipeline status, look at issues*

21. **What information do you wish GitLab surfaced more easily?**

    - *Examples: Who to ask about a piece of code, why a decision was made, what's blocking a release*

22. **How do you track what's happening across the team or project?** Do you look at:

    - MR activity
    - Issue boards
    - Milestone progress
    - Something else entirely

23. **What questions about your codebase or team's work would you love to be able to answer quickly?**

    - *Examples: "Who's the expert on this?", "What's our velocity?", "What's blocking this?"*

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 6: Collaboration & Communication (5 min)

*Goal: Understand how they work with others*

24. **Who do you collaborate with most? How does that usually work?**

25. **How do you typically communicate about code?** (MR comments, Slack, meetings, etc.)

26. **What's the handoff process like between you and other roles?** (design, PM, QA)

    - *Probe: What works well? What doesn't?*

27. **What skills have you developed most since starting here?**

    - *Probe: What helped you grow in those areas?*

28. **What do you want to get better at? What would help you grow in that direction?**

29. **If you could give advice to someone starting in your role tomorrow, what would you tell them?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Section 7: Ideal State (5 min)

*Goal: Understand what "better" looks like to them*

30. **If I could snap my fingers and fix one thing about how you work, what would it be?**

31. **What would it look like if you could spend more time on the work you actually enjoy?**

32. **Is there anything we didn't talk about that you think I should know?**

______________________________________________________________________

## Closing

> "This was really helpful, thanks for being so open. Based on what we learn from these conversations, we're hoping to build some tools or processes that actually help. If something comes up later that you think is relevant, feel free to ping me."

## Tips for Interviewers

1. **Listen more than you talk.** Aim for 80/20 - they should be talking 80% of the time.

2. **Use silence.** If they pause, don't jump in. Let them think - they'll often add something valuable.

3. **Follow the thread.** If they mention something interesting, dig deeper. The script is a guide, not a straitjacket.

4. **Capture exact quotes.** Their words are more powerful than your paraphrasing.

5. **Watch for emotions.** Frustration, excitement, resignation - these signal what really matters to them.

6. **Don't solve problems in the interview.** You're here to understand, not fix. Save solutions for later.

7. **Ask "why" and "can you give me an example" liberally.** These unlock the real insights.
